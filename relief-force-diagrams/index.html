Learn

Forces are shown with arrows

A force diagram shows every force acting on one object. Read everything on this page carefully. You will need all of it for the worksheet.

The five arrow rules

  1. Draw the object as a simple box or shape.
  2. Each arrow starts on the object.
  3. The arrow points in the direction of the force.
  4. A longer arrow means a bigger force.
  5. Label every arrow with the force's name (and its size in newtons, N, if you know it).

Forces are measured in newtons (N). An apple weighs about 1 N.

Example: pushing a box

The push arrow is twice as long as the friction arrow because the push (30 N) is twice as big as friction (15 N). Weight and support force are the same length because they are equal.

Forces you need to know

Weight

Gravity pulling the object down. Every object on Earth has weight, so almost every diagram has this arrow.

Support force

A surface (table, floor, ground) pushing up on an object resting on it.

Friction

Acts against the direction something is sliding or rolling. It slows things down.

Air resistance

Acts against an object moving through air. It gets bigger the faster you go. In water it is called water resistance.

Push, pull and driving force

A person or thing pushing or pulling. A rope or cord can pull too. A car's engine gives a driving force forwards.

Motion is NOT a force

Never draw an arrow just because something is moving. Only draw forces: pushes and pulls.

Balanced or unbalanced?

Balanced forces: the arrows in opposite directions are the same size. The motion does not change: a still object stays still, and a moving object keeps going at the same speed in the same direction.

Unbalanced forces: one side is bigger. The motion changes: the object speeds up, slows down or changes direction.

Watch out: a car cruising at a steady speed has balanced forces. Moving does not mean unbalanced!

Working out the overall force

When forces act in opposite directions, take the smaller one away from the bigger one. The overall force points the same way as the bigger force.

Push 30 N right, friction 15 N left
30 − 15 = 15 N to the right

If the forces are equal, the overall force is 0 N and they are balanced. The overall force is sometimes called the net force.

    Exit ticket

    A skateboarder is rolling to a stop on flat ground. Describe what their force diagram would look like (which arrows, which direction, which are equal). Then explain why there is no arrow pointing forwards.

    Model answer: Weight points down and the support force from the ground points up. These two arrows are the same length because they are balanced. A friction arrow points backwards, against the direction the skateboarder is rolling. There is no forward arrow because nothing is pushing them along. Motion is not a force, and the only sideways force is friction, so they slow down.
